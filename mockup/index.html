How it works

Three steps, and the secret never leaves your device.

1

Choose a passphrase

It never leaves your device. Argon2id (64 MiB, 3 passes) turns it into the key that unwraps your vault's master secret.

2

Everything is sealed locally

Passwords, notes and one-time-code seeds are encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 before they touch storage, in a versioned AegisEnvelopeV1.

3

Sync or export, still encrypted

Move between devices with an encrypted .aegis file, or let sync carry ciphertext that only your devices can open.

Security

We published the threat model, not a badge.

One passphrase derives every key you own. Passphrase changes re-wrap the master secret, so the vault is never re-encrypted in the clear, and the sync identity is a signing key that only an unlocked vault can produce.

  • Client-side WASM cryptography, auditable in the open
  • Sync relays and contracts see ciphertext only, never plaintext
  • Threat model names what we protect, and what we do not
  • Responsible disclosure policy and security.txt

Questions

Answered plainly.

Can Rinse Repeat Labs read my passwords?

No. Encryption and decryption happen on your device, in your browser. Our servers only ever hold ciphertext, and the key that opens it is derived from a passphrase we never receive.

What if I forget my passphrase?

Generate a recovery key in settings and store it offline. Without the passphrase or the recovery key the vault cannot be opened, by anyone, by design.

Do I need an account?

Not for the free vault. A billing account is only needed for paid sync and team features, and it never holds the key to your vault.

Is it really open source?

Yes, MIT or Apache-2.0, your choice. The browser vault, the crypto core, the sync contract and the documentation are all in the public repository.

What is Freenet mode?

An optional, experimental way to sync over a decentralised network with no relay at all. Most people should use the default browser vault and Personal sync.

Has Aegis been independently audited?

Not yet. The threat model and cryptography are published for review now, and an independent audit is planned before Teams leaves early access. We will link the report here when it exists.
